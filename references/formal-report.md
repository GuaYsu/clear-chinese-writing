# Formal report guidance

Apply these additional rules to lab reports, academic assignments, technical reports, research summaries, and similar formal prose.

## Report voice

Write as an author who understands the work, not as a commentator standing outside the report. Prefer concrete description of the method, observation, result, and reasoning.

Avoid defensive prose that anticipates every possible misunderstanding. A report should not sound as if it is arguing with an invisible reviewer.

Keep the register consistently formal. Do not import conversational first-person phrasing, chat-style asides, or abrupt shorthand merely because they sound natural in dialogue. Formal Chinese can still be fluent and idiomatic without becoming impersonal to the point of stiffness.

## Naturalness in formal prose

Do not over-compress sentences in the name of concision. Preserve the connective wording needed to make comparison, cause, scope, and sequence explicit.

A formal sentence should not read like a slide heading or meeting note. If a short predicate such as “更稳、更优、更合理、更清晰” carries an important judgment, state the comparison basis or reason when the context does not already make it obvious.

## Scope and limitations

A limitation is useful when it changes how a result should be interpreted. State:

- what is limited;
- by which condition, assumption, measurement, sample, or method;
- what inference therefore requires caution.

Do not add a generic “不能代表所有情况” sentence if no specific extrapolation issue exists.

## Results and discussion

Prefer this order when appropriate:

1. State the observed or calculated result.
2. Give the evidence or quantitative comparison.
3. Explain the mechanism or reason if supported.
4. State a limitation only if it affects interpretation.

Do not force a final “意义” sentence after every result.

## Method descriptions

Describe what was actually done. Do not inflate routine actions with ceremonial wording. Keep terminology stable and use equations, variables, units, and conditions precisely.

## Conclusions

A conclusion should synthesize results that have already been established. It should not introduce broader claims that the results do not support. If a short report has already made the conclusion obvious, keep the conclusion concise rather than repeating the entire discussion.

## Paragraphing

Use conventional prose paragraphs. A paragraph may contain several sentences that develop one point. Avoid one-sentence-per-line formatting, excessive bold text, and a heading for every minor thought.
