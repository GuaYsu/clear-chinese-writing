# Research basis for native-Chinese calibration

This note records the linguistic evidence behind the calibration rules. It is not default style context and should not be loaded for ordinary drafting.

- Corpus research on translated vs. original Chinese has found syntactic differences even without lexical cues. Reported tendencies in translated Chinese include more subject-position pronouns, more NP + “的” modifiers, determiners, and some coordinated structures. These are treated here as diagnostic signals, not errors.
  - Hai Hu, Wen Li, Sandra Kübler (2018), “Detecting Syntactic Features of Translated Chinese”, ACL Anthology: https://aclanthology.org/W18-1603/

- Research on Europeanized Chinese interpreting reports higher rates among novice than professional interpreters in verb nominalization, “一量名” patterns, overly long attributive modifiers, and judgment-sentence patterns. This supports checking sentence shape in addition to vocabulary.
  - Jing Zhou, Kanglong Liu, Jun Chu, corpus-based study on Europeanization in English-Chinese consecutive interpreting, PolyU Scholars Hub.

- Research on Chinese translationese and explicitation has documented increased or added use of connectives in some translated Chinese corpora. This supports checking whether logical relations have been marked more explicitly than the Chinese discourse actually needs.
  - Song Hua (2022), corpus-based study of connective explicitation in Chinese translations, Babel.

- Chinese collocation resources emphasize that lexical appropriateness depends on conventional word combinations, not only individual word meanings. Large-scale Chinese collocation projects have extracted verb-object and other relation types from corpora for language learning and error detection.
  - Hu, Chen, Chen (2016), “The Construction of a Chinese Collocational Knowledge Resource and Its Application for Second Language Acquisition”, COLING: https://aclanthology.org/C16-1307/
  - Wang, Rao, Xun (2021), large-scale modern Chinese verb-object collocation knowledge base based on BCC corpus.

- The Peking University CCL corpus provides large-scale original and translated Chinese corpora and can be used as an external plausibility check when a collocation is genuinely uncertain. Corpus frequency alone is not a style rule; genre and sense must still be considered.
  - https://cclcorpus.pku.edu.cn/

General caution: Europeanized constructions are part of modern Chinese and can enrich expression. The skill should target awkward transfer and overuse, not attempt to “purify” Chinese or eliminate structures merely because they have contact-induced histories.
