# Detailed revision checklist

Use this for a deliberate second-pass edit.

## Information
- Every sentence should contribute a fact, relation, reason, condition, implication, necessary stance, or useful transition.
- Remove sentences whose only job is to sound complete, cautious, important, or academic.
- Do not treat all rhetorical function as empty content. A comparison frame or transition can be necessary even when it adds no new fact.

## Contrast and negation
- Search for “不是、并非、而非、不能代表、不意味着、不使用、与其说、不如说”.
- Keep them only when the contrast, disambiguation, boundary, or exclusion itself matters.
- If the sentence can state the intended point directly without loss, prefer the direct version.

## Naturalness and compression
- Read for phrases that are technically grammatical but sound like translated headlines, meeting notes, or slide labels.
- Check whether concision has removed a needed comparison frame, subject relation, qualification, or connective phrase.
- Be cautious with repeated shorthand predicates such as “更稳、更轻、更硬、更顺、更干净”. Keep them when context makes them natural; otherwise state what they mean.
- When shorter and more idiomatic versions conflict, choose the idiomatic version unless the genre specifically demands telegraphic brevity.

## Register
- Identify the intended register before editing: conversational discussion, explanatory prose, or formal finished writing.
- Check for abrupt switching between “我觉得 / 我更倾向于” and impersonal specification language.
- Do not add first-person phrasing simply to make the text feel human.
- When revising the user's text, preserve broad voice characteristics without copying accidental fillers or errors.

## Evidence
- Replace vague evaluation with numbers, comparisons, criteria, observations, or mechanisms where available.
- Do not fabricate quantitative support that the source does not provide.

## Logic
- Check each “因此、然而、此外、与此同时、由此可见、综上” for a real logical role.
- Remove transitions that merely decorate adjacency.
- Keep ordinary connective language when it genuinely helps the reader follow comparison, cause, sequence, or qualification.

## Terminology
- Use one stable term per concept unless a distinction is intended.
- Do not introduce synonyms merely for variety.

## Sentence construction
- Prefer active, direct predicates when natural in Chinese.
- Reduce empty shells such as “进行、开展、实现、完成、加以”.
- Do not force a direct-verb rewrite if it produces clipped or unnatural Chinese.
- Split sentences that carry too many independent logical tasks, but do not reduce prose to choppy fragments.

## Paragraphs
- One paragraph should develop one coherent unit of thought.
- Merge related single-sentence paragraphs.
- Split only when the topic, stage of reasoning, or rhetorical function genuinely changes.

## Endings
- Remove forced “意义、基础、参考、展望” endings unless concretely supported.
- A paragraph or section may stop after the useful information is complete.

## Final voice check
- The text should sound like someone who knows what they mean and says it naturally.
- It should not sound like someone trying to prove that they are being formal, balanced, careful, sophisticated, or human.
- Read the whole paragraph, not only individual sentences. A series of individually acceptable sentences can still feel tonally discontinuous.

## Native reconstruction
- If a phrase remains awkward after one or two local substitutions, stop patching it word by word. Restate the intended meaning in a fresh Chinese sentence.
- Check whether a sentence's structure looks inherited from an abstract English-like frame even when every individual Chinese word is correct.
- Keep useful rhetorical material such as comparison frames, modal softening, or conjunction pairs when they make the relation more natural.
- Read for cadence across the whole sentence and paragraph; do not reduce every sentence to the same compact shape.
- Check for over-polished commentary diction. Words such as “固然、绝非、归根结底、尤为、着眼于、精准、颇具” are legitimate, but repeated use may create a new formulaic style.
- Prefer plain, idiomatic modern Chinese when it carries the same meaning without loss.
